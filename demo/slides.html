Streaming pipeline

Reads one month at a time and aggregates to 17,284 land tiles of 1°. Quality checks passed.

Test
Calibrated trend tests

OLS trend; p is the larger of an AR(1)-corrected and an annual-means test (calibrated to 3–6% false positives), then Benjamini–Hochberg FDR.

→
Map
Spatial clusters

Local Moran’s I with permutation tests finds coherent drying and wetting regions, not lone pixels.

Discovery Cards

7 cards, each with a robustness checklist, a named user and decision, and its caveats.

Without a gap-free, globally consistent record there is nothing to split. GLDAS-2.1 gives the same monthly grid over every land surface for 2000–2025, so a trend in Chile and a trend in Siberia are measured the same way.

The precipitation variable (Rainf_f_tavg) is GLDAS model forcing derived from GPCP. We call results “detectable trends” and “associated with”, never causes.

The detective that checks itself

Paradox Earth caught a false trend: it lines up with changes in the model’s input data.

Soil water was our original headline: a 26-year global decline. Our own break test found level shifts at 2010-08 and 2024-07, matching documented changes in GLDAS radiation forcing; inside the consistent window 2012-01 to 2024-06 the decline is not significant.

RETRACTED
Soil water 0–200 cm · original headline

A step is not a trend. The break test lined the jump up with a documented change in GLDAS-2.1 radiation forcing (GES DISC README), so the soil-water decline was withdrawn instead of published.

Who this protects

Teachers and students using the Paradox Earth explorer: Whether to trust a dramatic trend line before checking how the data were made. A tool that rejects its own most exciting result shows why every trend needs a homogeneity check, not just a p-value.

Rigor as a feature: the tool rejects its own result when the evidence does not hold.6 / 7
